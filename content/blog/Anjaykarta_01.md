# Soft-Launching My Own TTRPG System: Anjaykarta!

My last entry to the [Indonesian Blogwagon!](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/indonesian-ttrpg-blogwagon/)

**What is Anjaykarta?** I like to define Anjaykarta as a hack of _Electric Bastionland_ combined with _Blades in the Dark_'s clock-driven faction system. It's a fast-paced, low-prep urban roleplaying game designed to capture the gritty, chaotic, and hyper-capitalist survival experience of a fictionalized Jakarta.

## The Standout Features

- **Finding "Walking Wallets" as a Goal:** Similar to EBL, our players need to settle their debt. The difference? Your group is saddled with a massive, shared debt of exactly 100 million Rupiah. The only way to survive is to hunt down elusive "Walking Wallets" wealthy benefactors and Sugar Daddies who have enough capital to wipe your ledger clean before your time runs out.
- **Hinges Around a Core Triangle:** Every decision forces a continuous trade-off between **Time**, **Money**, and **Safety** (a physical step-die that degrades from a d10 down to a deadly d4 when taking risks).
- **Concrete Pointcrawl:** There are no grid maps here. Travel is abstracted across five massive City States, each governed by Wealth, Danger, and Traffic stats. Navigating to a building means rolling on a _Kecamatan_ table to instantly generate local street flavor.
- **Streamlined Three-Tier Economy:** Everything from weapons and clinics to black markets operates on an intuitive 1-to-3 tier system. Tier 3 is cheap street-level grit, while Tier 1 is high-end luxury heavily gatekept by wealth or faction influence.
- **Factions as a Living City:** Players interact with local _Ormas_ and syndicates, ticking _Compliance_ clocks for favors or _Heat_ clocks when they inevitably choose violence.

## Why make Anjaykarta?

I have three main reasons why I ultimately decided to create this system.

**1. A Gateway for My Local Friends** 

I really love inviting my friends into my hobbies, but TTRPGs can have a tough barrier to entry. You need a dedicated group, a Dungeon Master, specific dice, and hours just to learn the rules. Most people recognize _Dungeons & Dragons_ as the entry point, but it has a massive learning curve and a Eurocentric fantasy setting that not everyone resonates with. I wanted to reduce this cognitive load. What better way to get immersed quickly than using your own city? Anjaykarta was made so Indonesians can recognize the details instantly. Even the "Failed Career" options—like the _Calon CPNS_ or the _Mantan Jukir Liar_—reflect everyday Indonesian life. I hope players can step into the shoes of everyday people and see Jakarta through a new lens.

**2. Introducing Foreigners to the Real Jakarta**

Let's be honest: Indonesia's cultural export is usually either incredibly weird (like the Tung Tung Sahur memes) or completely obscure.  There is a massive gap in how the rest of the world sees us. Hell, some people know Bali more than Indonesia itself. Furthermore, all we're famous for is for bad rep these days, such as the infamous MBG poisoning. This isn't always the case. I want this TTRPG to act as a pavement for foreigners to truly experience what it's like to navigate this city. Instead of your eurocentric taverns, they get to experience the sheer chaos, the sprawling traffic, and the vibrant street-food culture of this hyper-capitalist concrete circus.

**3. Discovering the City for Myself** 

Anjaykarta is ultimately is a personal project. I grew up here in this city, but I haven't really gotten the chance to explore the depths of Jakarta when I was little. I was just doing my commute and didn't really realize the depths of the city. Now that I have the freedom, building this game is my excuse to dive deep into the city's veins. From researching the elite hangouts in Jakarta Selatan to the heavy container truck traffic of Jakarta Utara, discovering weird organizations, down to the sweet, little mundane details about my neighbourhood, writing this system is my way of learning more about Jakarta and, ultimately, learning to love it for all its flaws.

## What are the design philosophy?

As I said above, I want to make a game where the system is ridiculously easy to learn but the GM has so many tools to enrich the world itself. I've been trying to strip down EBL to the basics to make it accessible. I need several more playtest though to see if Anjaykarta's stripped down approach is better or worse for beginners.

Another bizarre thing I added is the 3-tier system. This feels very videogamey, and far from OSR, but for good reasons. I decided to introduce this mechanic because to make it easy to categorize which items are good and which ones are better. Having too much freedom or imagination makes it hard to ground yourself, on how can you utilize this item or understand the value. The three tier system aims to streamline that process of worth evaluating in almost every aspect for Anjaykarta. Of course, tiers doesn't mean like it's a strict system like Borderlands, and I don't want tiers to restrict the storytelling. I feel like tiers can help differentiate the experience on different regions and factions, to make the GM easier to tell the experiences apart.

Also! Safety! I want to introduce a different random encounter mechanic that hinges on each individual stats instead of a universal one. Think of...Yakuza's random goons system, but make it coop. 

## What do I learn so far?

Anjaykarta started out as a system me and my good friend CAN (illustrator for my Appendix N entry btw!!!) for her birthday. And since I wanted to make a simple system, I thought that making a hack of an already simple system would suffice. 

I was wrong.

By untangling EBL I understood that although simple, this system is very elaborate in it's details. I find it *very* impressive that EBL is made this impressive. I've been reading so many systems in writing Anjaykarta (30 systems~) and my god, creating a system is harder not because it contains so many words but the philosophy built around it. I'm probably going to need another blogpost to dissect EBL's genius...

## What's next?

The system is *far* from done. Currently, I'm writing both the English and Indonesian version, so doing double the work is very tiring. Moreover, I myself am also still refining the Faction and misc system in Jaykarta. We plan to fully release somewhere in 2027, so until then, we'll be posting updates and WIPs in our itch and social media. Thank you for reading!
